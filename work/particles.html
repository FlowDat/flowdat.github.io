Turbulent dispersion

Inertial particle-pair diffusion

How massive particles, not tracers, separate in a turbulent flow — and what that means for pollen, sand, and industrial aerosols.

Thesis
KFUPM Ph.D., 2019 — kinematic simulation of inertial particle-pair diffusion
Talks
AMMCS 2017 · EGU 2017
With
Nadeem A. Malik
Method
Kinematic simulation (KS)

Tracers follow the fluid. Inertial particles do not. Their Stokes number decides whether they ride eddies or cut through them, and pair dispersion — how two particles forget each other — changes accordingly. Richardson’s 1926 locality picture is the textbook; a non-local theory of pair diffusion, developed with Nadeem Malik, is the alternative we tested.

Kinematic simulation builds a synthetic turbulence that is cheap enough to track millions of pairs, yet rich enough in scales to distinguish local from non-local spreading. Low-St particles behave like dyes. High-St particles filter the small eddies and remember ballistic stretches. The applications are not abstract: pollen in a canopy, sand in a haboob, droplets in a spray, catalysts in a reactor.

This half of the KFUPM dissertation sits beside the 3DS DNS. One chapter asks how to generate turbulence. The other asks how matter, once released, occupies it.
