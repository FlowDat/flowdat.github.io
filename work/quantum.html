Quantum molecular dynamics

Stair-shaped grids for reactive scattering

Hyperspherical wavepackets that stop wasting points on regions the reaction never visits — and still resolve long-lived resonances.

Papers
Chem. Phys. Lett. 837, 141060 (2024)
Theor. Chem. Acc. 143, 61 (2024)
DOI
10.1016/j.cplett.2023.141060
10.1007/s00214-024-03133-w
With
Umair Umer, Syed Kazim Usman, Hailin Zhao, Zhigang Sun
Lab
State Key Laboratory of Molecular Reaction Dynamics, DICP

Three atoms meet; a bond breaks; another forms. In hyperspherical coordinates the whole story — reactants, products, every arrangement in between — lives on one map. That elegance has a cost. As the hyper-radius grows, the angular grid must densify, and long-range potentials or long absorption tails make the calculation prohibitive.

We split the hyper-radius into stair-shaped domains. Each step keeps its own number of angular points. Regions where the energy is too high for the reaction to live are simply not stored. The wavepacket still sees a continuous physics; the computer sees a staircase.

Reactions, and the saving

On F + HCl the stair grid cut the mesh by about 18% against the usual method, with results matching established techniques. O + O₂ saved nearly 30%. Cl + H₂ more than 33%. The 2024 Theoretical Chemistry Accounts paper then takes the same idea to product state-resolved probabilities, including long-lived resonances that stretch over long-range grids — the regime where a naïve time-dependent method usually fails.

This is the molecular counterpart of the 3DS story: do not pay for points that do not carry the physics.
