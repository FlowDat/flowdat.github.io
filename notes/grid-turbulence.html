DNS of grid-generated turbulence

Classical, fractal, and 3D sparse grids. A burst-buffer algorithm that makes the Navier–Stokes comparison affordable.

Journal
Computers & Fluids 308 (2026) 106985
DOI
10.1016/j.compfluid.2026.106985
Earlier
J. Phys.: Conf. Series 1101, 012048 (2018)
Code
FlowDat/3DS

Grid-generated turbulence turns a uniform stream into a controllable cascade. Classical square grids do it at one scale. Flat fractal grids inject several scales at once, at the cost of high blockage. The 3D sparse grid (3DS) puts each scale on its own co-frame, spaced along the tunnel, so effective blockage can drop well below a 2D fractal of similar intensity (Malik, US Patent 9,599,269 B2).

Direct numerical simulation of the full grid, the developing wakes, and the downstream decay is I/O-bound. The burst-buffer accelerated DNS algorithm (BBDSA) parks those bursts on fast intermediate storage. The 2026 Computers & Fluids study reports a fivefold cut in wall time and an eightfold gain in parallel efficiency, with no loss of accuracy.

Comparison at Re = 4000

Uniform inflow in a laboratory-scale conduit. Two-dimensional classical grids, two-dimensional fractal grids, and 3DSG. A 3DSG at 24% blockage produced turbulence intensities and Reynolds stresses comparable to classical and fractal grids with substantially higher blockage: the same free-stream turbulence for less drag.

Fractal grids generate intermittent multi-scale wakes and slower decay through continuous energy injection. Classical grids decay more predictably at a single dominant scale. Sparse 3D grids sit between them: sustained multi-scale activity with improved homogeneity, which is the mixing argument for combustion and chemical reactors.

The 2018 open-access DNS (J. Phys.: Conf. Series 1101, 012048) mapped the three-frame parameter space — blockage, effective mesh size, inter-frame spacing. Wind-tunnel fractal wakes were reported the same year in Topical Problems of Fluid Mechanics.
