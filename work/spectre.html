SPECTRE — a scale-aware emulator of turbulent mixing layers

Direct numerical simulation remains the truth. SPECTRE is the fast twin: a physics-constrained network that reconstructs mixing layers from Re 2,500 to 30,000.

Mixing layers are simple enough to DNS and hard enough to matter: two streams, a shear, a scalar, a growing thickness. They are also a brutal test for a surrogate. A network that only fits one Reynolds number has learned a picture, not a flow.

SPECTRE is a scale-separated, physics-constrained deep network trained on DNS of turbulent mixing layers at seven Reynolds numbers — 2,500, 5,000, 10,000, 15,000, 20,000, 25,000, 30,000. The fields stored with the model are streamwise and wall-normal velocity, pressure, and scalar concentration, together with the mean, the temporal coefficients of a reduced-order model, and the reconstruction. The public analysis stack plots spectral content, stresses, coherent structures, and mixing metrics against the original DNS.

The companion manuscript, Scale-separated, physics constrained, deep neural network for turbulent mixing layer simulations, is under review at Results in Engineering (2026). The SMLG repository is the working digital twin of that paper: copy the Zenodo dump into data/, run the analysis, and the figures of the manuscript come back.

The point of a twin

DNS of a mixing layer at Re 30,000 is not something you rerun to try a new Schmidt number over lunch. An emulator that respects scale separation and the conservation structure of the flow is how you explore the neighbourhood of a validated case — and how a platform like HydroTwin can feel interactive without lying about the physics.
